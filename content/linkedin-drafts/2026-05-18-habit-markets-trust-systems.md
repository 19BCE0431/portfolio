## Hook

In habitual categories, the real competitor is often not another brand. It is the customer's comfort with what already feels safe.

## Short Context

While working through an atta case study, I realized that the category was less simple than it first appeared.

Atta is not just a packaged product in many Indian homes. It sits inside daily routine, family preference, and a quiet fear of getting an everyday meal wrong.

## Main Insight

That means growth cannot depend only on attention. A campaign can create recall, but a staple category needs repeated trust signals: availability, familiarity, consistency, retail confidence, and a lower perceived risk of trial.

## Crisp Points

- Consumers in daily-use categories often optimize for least regret, not maximum novelty.
- Retailers are not passive channels. They can quietly shape trust.
- Switching becomes easier when the brand feels familiar before the first purchase.
- The best strategy may not break habit immediately. It may work with inertia first.
