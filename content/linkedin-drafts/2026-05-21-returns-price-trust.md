## Hook

Your return policy is a pricing decision.

It’s the only pricing decision most brands hide on a help page.

## Short Context

Returns are usually discussed like an operations problem: reverse logistics, refund tickets, warehouse flow.

But the customer reads the policy *before* purchase as a risk signal.

In categories with fit uncertainty and trust asymmetry (especially in e-commerce), that risk signal can matter as much as the listed price.

## Main Insight

A generous return policy is an insurance offer: you’re selling an option to be wrong. That option increases willingness-to-try (and sometimes willingness-to-pay), but it also attracts behaviors you may not have underwritten (bracketing, serial returns, low-intent browsing). The strategy is not “strict vs lenient.” It’s deciding *who* gets the insurance, *when*, and *what it costs the system*.

## Crisp Points

- “Free returns” shifts downside risk from customer → business. That is why it lifts conversion.
- If you don’t segment that risk transfer, customers will: chronic returners arbitrage generosity.
- Return rate is often created upstream (product accuracy, marketing promises, assortment clarity), not in the warehouse.
- A strong returns strategy is four parts: risk allocation, fair friction, disposition economics, and a prevention loop.
