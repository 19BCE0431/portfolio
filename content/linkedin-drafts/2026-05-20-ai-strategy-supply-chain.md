## Final LinkedIn Post

Most AI strategy decks still miss the most boring question in the room:

What is the supply chain of intelligence?

Not the model demo.
Not the chatbot.
Not another "AI transformation" slide.

The supply chain.

Every useful AI workflow now depends on things that behave like operating inputs:

- compute capacity
- clean context
- latency budgets
- data permissions
- cost per decision
- escalation rules
- feedback loops

This changes the AI conversation.

If intelligence has a supply chain, then "use AI everywhere" is not strategy. It is a cost leak with better branding.

A customer-support issue may deserve fast, high-context intelligence.

A weekly market scan may not.

A pricing alert may need competitor movement, margin, inventory, category role, and seasonality before it deserves action.

A content workflow may need brand memory more than a larger model.

A dashboard may need fewer charts and a clearer next step.

The question becomes:

Which decisions are valuable enough to spend intelligence on?

Businesses already ration attention, capital, shelf space, inventory, and leadership time.

AI adds one more scarce resource to allocate: structured cognition.

Coming from Computer Science and Data Science into an MBA, this is the shift I find most interesting.

The technical question is:
"Can the system do it?"

The business question is:
"Should this decision receive this much intelligence, speed, cost, and autonomy?"

I wrote the longer version here:
https://mohitsaikrishna.in/journal/ai-strategy-supply-chain?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=post&utm_campaign=linkedin_journal_first_post

Maybe the next AI advantage will not belong to the team that automates the most.

It may belong to the team that knows where intelligence is worth spending.
