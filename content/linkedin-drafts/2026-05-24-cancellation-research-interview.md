## Hook

Most churn dashboards are built on polite lies.

The truth shows up at the cancel button.

## Short Context

Cancellation is usually treated as a retention moment: “save the revenue.”

But it’s also one of the highest-signal user research moments your product gets, because the user is making a real decision with real stakes.

## Main Insight

When teams add friction to cancellation, they don’t just lose goodwill.

They corrupt churn diagnosis.

People don’t give you better reasons when they’re trapped in a flow. They pick whatever gets them out fastest (“too expensive”), or they switch channels (support, reviews, chargebacks). The result: the dashboard looks clean, but the learning loop gets noisy.

## Crisp Points

- Cancellation is a measurement instrument, not just UX.
- Friction creates bad exit data + higher-cost channels (support/disputes).
- Design for “exit integrity”: simple cancel mechanics + consent-based save offers.
- Capture reasons with low effort (one tap first, optional text second).
- Measure downstream: time-to-cancel, drop-off in cancel flow, post-cancel tickets, disputes, and “save regret.”
