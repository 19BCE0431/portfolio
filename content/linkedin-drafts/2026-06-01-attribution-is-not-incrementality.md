## Hook

Attribution doesn’t tell you what worked.

It tells you who got the credit.

## Short Context

This is why a team can have “great ROAS” and still not be growing.

Attribution is answering a different question than the one leaders think they’re asking.

## Main Insight

ROAS dashboards are credit-allocation systems built from observed paths. They’re useful for optimizing inside a channel — but they’re not a reliable measure of what your spend *caused*. Incrementality is a counterfactual question (“what would have happened without this campaign?”), which is why lift tests (holdouts, geo splits, etc.) are the right tool when you’re deciding where the next chunk of budget should go.

## Crisp Points

- **Separate two decisions:** optimization *within* a channel vs allocation *across* channels.
- **Use platform ROAS tactically:** iterate creative/audiences; don’t treat it as causal truth.
- **Anchor on blended outcomes:** blended CAC/payback + new customer rate are harder to game.
- **Run periodic lift tests:** one high-confidence holdout per quarter beats weekly dashboard debates.
- **Watch for the trap:** channels that harvest existing intent can look “efficient” while starving long-term demand creation.
