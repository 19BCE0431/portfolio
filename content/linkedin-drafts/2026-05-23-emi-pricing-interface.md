## Hook

Your price tag is not ₹49,999.

It’s ₹2,999/month.

## Short Context

In India, EMI/BNPL isn’t just a way to pay. It’s increasingly the *way the price is understood* at the decision moment.

Once customers compare monthly outflow instead of total cost, checkout stops being a payments choice. It becomes a pricing interface.

## Main Insight

That framing shift edits the unit of comparison - and units matter more than we admit. You’re no longer competing only on product vs product. You’re competing against the customer’s monthly commitments. And even when MRP doesn’t move, “no-cost EMI” still has a cost somewhere (fees, subvention, margin, promo budget). If your discounting discipline only tracks coupons, you can miss the biggest leak: the checkout itself quietly becomes the discount.

## Crisp Points

- **EMI changes valuation:** customers ask “Can I live with ₹Y/month?” instead of “Is it worth ₹X?”
- **EMI changes your funnel:** it can lift conversion + AOV, but also shifts who you attract (and what support load follows).
- **EMI is underwriting:** defaults, tenure options, and which SKUs are financeable are strategy decisions.
- **Measure the full loop:** repeat rate, post-purchase friction (cancels/returns/disputes), and net margin after financing - not just conversion.
