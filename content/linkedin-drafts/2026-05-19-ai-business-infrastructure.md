## Final LinkedIn Post

AI is starting to look less like a software category and more like business infrastructure.

The question is no longer only:
"Which AI tool should we try?"

It is becoming:
"Which parts of our operating model can become AI-native?"

The visible layer is compute: cloud, data centers, chips, and model capacity.

But the more interesting layer is inside the business:

- customer operations that can understand, route, and resolve faster
- pricing systems that read market movement earlier
- product discovery that connects intent to the right answer
- marketing workflows that learn from campaigns, not just generate copy
- dashboards that move from passive reporting to decision support

That is where agentic AI becomes important.

Not because every workflow should run on autopilot.
But because many repeated workflows can be redesigned with context, tools, rules, and human review built in.

I have seen a smaller version of this in data science and automation work: anomaly alerts, price intelligence, document extraction, search, dashboards, and AI workflows become valuable only when they connect to a real business decision.

Now, as an MBA student, this is the question I find most interesting:

AI adoption is easy to announce.
AI-native operating models are much harder to build.
